3 Liability for personal injury or damage to property within ICE Conditions of Contract

As discussed in Chapter 2, it is an unfortunate consequence of engineering construction that death or personal injury can occur or that loss of and damage to property can result. Those who may be liable can include the Employer, main Contractor, Sub Contractors, the Engineer or other professionals. Liability can occur due to negligence, contractual breach, statutory breach or otherwise, as previously described. The ICE Conditions of Contract generally refer to liabilities to ‘any person’, which would include third parties, and Employer’s and Contractor’s employees. Those employees who are in the United Kingdom are covered by compulsory Employer’s Liability insurance — as discussed in Chapter 7 later. The ICE Conditions of Contract include clauses which state which risks are for which of the contracting parties and what provisions must be made by a Contractor for that Contractor’s portion of those risks. Requirements are usually that a Contractor provides an indemnity to an Employer, reinforced by insurance.

The following paragraphs describe how liability risks are divided between an Employer and a Contractor, and the indemnities required in the ICE Conditions of Contract current at the time of writing. Using the abbreviations defined at the beginning of this text, these are:

  • ICE 6;
  • ICE Design and Construct;
  • ICE Minor Works;
  • NEC.

ICE 6, Clause 22

Wording of Clause 22(1) — Damage to persons and property

‘The Contractor shall except if and so far as the Contract provides otherwise and subject to the exceptions set out in sub-clause (2) of this Clause indemnify and keep indemnified the Employer against all losses and claims in respect of

  1. (a) death of or injury to any person or
  2. (b) loss of or damage to any property (other than the Works)
which may arise out of or in consequence of the design construction and completion of the Works and the remedying of any defects therein and against all claims demands proceedings damages costs charges and expenses whatsoever in respect thereof or in relation thereto’.

Commentary on Clause 22(1)

The effect of Clause 22(1) is that a Contractor gives an Employer a very wide indemnity against death or injury to persons or loss or damage to property, and against all legal proceedings, costs and charges connected thereto.

The words in the first line, ‘except if and so far as the Contract provides otherwise’, emphasises that the Contract should be read as a whole, since words elsewhere in the Contract may qualify the indemnity. They are particularly referring to Clause 22(2), Exceptions (see below).

A slight confusion is that the Contractor must indemnify the Employer ‘against death or injury to any person …’ and this wording must include Contractor’s employees. However, Clause 24 deals with Contractor’s employees and will normally apply, as it is the more specific clause.

Wording of Clause 22(2) — Exceptions

‘The exceptions referred to in sub-clause (1) of this Clause which are the responsibility of the Employer are

  1. (a) damage to crops being on the Site (save in so far as possession has not been given to the Contractor)
  2. (b) the use or occupation of land provided by the Employer for the purposes of the Contract (including consequent losses of crops) or interference whether temporary or permanent with any right of way light air or water or other easement or quasi-easement which is the unavoidable result of the construction of the Works in accordance with the Contract
  3. (c) the right of the Employer to construct the Works or any part thereof on over under in or through any land
  4. (d) damage which is the unavoidable result of the construction of the Works in accordance with the Employer’s Requirements including any design for which the Contractor is not responsible under the Contract and
  5. (e) death of or injury to persons or loss of or damage to property resulting from any act neglect or breach of statutory duty done or committed by the Employer his agents servants or other contractors (not being employed by the Contractor) or for or in respect of any claims demands proceedings damages costs charges and expenses in respect thereof or in relation thereto’.

Commentary on Clause 22(2)

The indemnity does not apply to the following, which are the Employer’s responsibility:

  1. (a) ‘damage to crops being on the Site’
  2. (b) ‘the use or occupation of land … or interference … with any right of way light air or water or other easement … which is the unavoidable result of the construction of the Works’.

    3. The foregoing is logical since if such forms of injury or damage are unavoidable it is unreasonable for a Contractor to be responsible for them. Not only that, a Contractor cannot obtain insurance against liability for unavoidable injury or damage, since any such insurance will be restricted to accidental injury or damage. An easement is a right over land, such as a right to remove crops or fruit, enjoyed by the owner of adjoining land.

  3. (c) ‘the right of the Employer to construct the Works … on over under in or through any land’. This is a logical exemption also since it is an Employer’s responsibility to make sure that the Employer has good title to the land upon which the contract is to be performed and has adequately checked all deeds, leases and planning permission.
  4. (d) ‘damage which is the unavoidable result of the construction of the Works in accordance with the Employer’s Requirements’ is another sensible exemption. If such damage is unavoidable then it is a risk for an Employer. It will be noted that the reference is to damage only, not personal injuries. It will be realised that what constitutes ‘unavoidable’ can almost always be a matter of dispute; for example, an Employer can state that Works could have been done differently albeit more expensively. In practice it is often difficult for a Contractor not to be judged liable under this Clause.
  5. (e) ‘death … injury … or loss of or damage … resulting from any act neglect or breach of statutory duty … by the Employer his agents servants or other contractors (not being employed by the Contractor)’. It would clearly be unfair to make a Contractor responsible for the acts of an Employer over which a Contractor has no control.

Wording of Clause 22(3) — Indemnity by Employer

‘The Employer shall subject to sub-clause (4) of this Clause indemnify the Contractor against all claims demands proceedings damages costs charges and expenses in respect of the matters referred to in the exceptions defined in sub-clause (2) of this Clause’.

Commentary on Clause 22(3)

In this Clause, an Employer indemnifies a Contractor for the risks the Employer has accepted, as defined in Clause 22(2). These are the first four risks listed in that Clause, presumably because they each deal with forms of inevitable or unavoidable damage against which a Contractor could not obtain insurance and in fairness should not be liable for anyhow. The Clause may only apply to a proportion of the fifth risk listed as covered by Clause 22(4), as follows.

Wording of Clause 22(4) — Shared responsibility

  1. (a) The Contractor’s liability to indemnify the Employer under subclause (1) of this Clause shall be reduced in proportion to the extent that the act or neglect of the Employer his agents servants or other contractors (not being employed by the Contractor) may have contributed to the said death injury loss or damage.

  2. (b) The Employer’s liability to indemnify the Contractor under subclause (3) of this Clause in respect of matters referred to in subclause (2)(e) of this Clause shall be reduced in proportion to the extent that the act or neglect of the Contractor or his subcontractors servants or agents may have contributed to the said death injury loss or damage’.

Commentary on Clause 22(4)

Clause 22(4)(c) reduces a Contractor’s liability in proportion to the extent that an Employer and those engaged by an Employer may be responsible for death, injury, loss or damage. In other words, if an Employer is 60% responsible, a Contractor will only be liable for the remaining 40%.

Clause 22(4)(b) reduces an Employer’s liability in the same way for the fifth exemption listed in Clause 22(2).

ICE Design and Construct, Clause 22

Except for a change in Clause title to ‘Damage to persons or property’, and an addition to the Exceptions given in Clause 22(2)(d) to include ‘any design for which the Contractor is not responsible under the Contract’, there is no difference in the wording between Clause 22 of ICE Design and Construct and Clause 22 of ICE 6. Clause 22(1) in conjunction with Clause 22(2)(d) confirms that a Contractor must indemnify an Employer against losses which arise as a consequence of the Contractor’s design of the permanent as well as temporary Works.

ICE Minor Works,Clauses 10.2 to 10.5

Wording of Clause 10.2 — Contractor to indemnify the Employer

‘The Contractor shall indemnify and keep the Employer indemnified against all losses and claims for injury or damage to any person or property whatsoever (save for the matters for which the Contractor is responsible under Clause 3.2) which may arise out of or in consequence of the Works and against all claims demands proceedings damages costs charges and expenses whatsoever in respect thereof or in relation thereto subject to Clauses 10.3 and 10.4’.

Wording of Clause 10.3

‘The liability of the Contractor to indemnify the Employer under Clause 10.2 shall be reduced proportionately to the extent that the act or neglect of the Engineer or the Employer his servants or agents or other contractors not employed by the Contractor may have contributed to the said loss injury or damage’.

Wording of Clause 10.4

‘The Contractor shall not be liable for or in respect of or to indemnify the Employer against any compensation or damage for or with respect to:

  1. (a) damage to crops being on the Site (save in so far as possession has not been given to the Contractor)
  2. (b) the use or occupation of land (which has been provided by the Employer) by the Works or any part thereof or for the purpose of constructing completing and maintaining the Works (including consequent loss of crops) or interference whether temporary or permanent with any right of way light air or water or other easement or quasi-easement which are the unavoidable result of the construction of the Works in accordance with the Contract
  3. (c) the right of the Employer to construct the Works or any part thereof on over under in or through any land
  4. (d) damage which is the unavoidable result of the construction of the Works in accordance with the Contract
  5. (e) death or injury to persons or loss of or damage to property resulting from any act or neglect or breach of statutory duty done or committed by the Engineer or the Employer his agents servants or other contractors (not being employed by the Contractor) or for or in respect of any claims demands proceedings damages costs charges and expenses in respect thereof or in relation thereto’.

Wording of Clause 10.5 — Employer to indemnify Contractor

‘The Employer shall save harmless and indemnify the Contractor from and against all claims demands proceedings damages costs charges and expenses in respect of the matters referred to in Clause 10.4. Provided always that the Employer’s liability to indemnify the Contractor under paragraph (e) of Clause 10.4 shall be reduced proportionately to the extent that the act or neglect of the Contractor or his sub-contractors servants or agents may have contributed to the said injury or damage’.

Commentary on Clauses 10.2 to 10.5

The relevant wordings of ICE Minor Works are an update of the words in ICE 6. The end result is somewhat clearer but the actual effect is little different.

NEC, Clauses 80.1, 81.1, 83.1 and 83.2

In this and subsequent sections, Clauses in NEC are not necessarily described in the clause order in which they appear in that form of contract. This has been done to provide a simpler comparison with similar topics in other forms covered by this text.

NEC, Clause 81 — Contractor’s risks

Wording of Clause 81.1

‘From the starting date until the Defects Certificate has been issued the risks which are not carried by the Employer are carried by the Contractor.

NEC,Clause 80 — Employer’s risks

Part-wording of Clause 80.1

‘The Employer’s risks are

  • — Claims, proceedings, compensation and costs payable which are due to
    • — use or occupation of the Site by the works or for the purpose of the workswhich is the unavoidable result of the works,
    • — negligence, breach of statutory duty or interference with any legal right by the Employeror by any person employed by or contracted to him except the Contractor or
    • — a fault of the Employer or a fault in his design.

and:

  • — Additional Employer’s risks stated in the Contract Data’.

NEC, Clause 83 — Indemnity

Wording of Clause 83.1

‘Each party indemnifies the other against claims, proceedings, compensation and costs due to an event which is at his risk.’

Wording of Clause 83.2

‘The liability of each Party to indemnify the other is reduced if events at the other Party’s risk contributed to the claims, proceedings, compensation and costs. The reduction is in proportion to the extent that events which were at the other Party’s risk contributed, taking into account each Party’s responsibilities under this contract.’

Commentary on Clauses 80.1, 81.1, 83.1 and 83.2

There is no specific reference to liability for personal injury, death, or loss of or damage to property. However, it is the clear intention that such matters are included within Clauses 80.1 and 81.1. The omission of specific words would, however, appear to give the opportunity to widen the scope of the Clauses and it remains to be seen what other issues might be considered to be included within this wording.

The term ‘unavoidable’ remains with its potential for dispute as described earlier, and covers personal injury or death, in addition to damage to property.

The Employer’s risks being ‘defined’, all other risks are the Contractor’s. Besides the listed risks, the Employer has the opportunity to add other risks which may, for instance, be project-specific (this would be achieved by special conditions of contract or by contractual agreement when using other standard forms of contract). Scope is presumably unlimited; for example, it could cover the risk of flooding above a certain level, all losses on a particular section of the works, and so on.

Clauses 83.1 and 83.2 deal clearly with the extent of indemnification in proportion to the risks for which each party is responsible.
