9 Requirement for Works insurance within ICE Conditions of Contract

The ICE Conditions of Contract state whether or not a Contractor is required to insure his/her responsibilities for loss or damage to contract works. In the event of serious loss or damage, where insurance exists both a Contractor and an Employer then know that a sum of money should be available from an insurance policy which will enable the Contractor to repair or make good such loss or damage. In the absence of insurance, a Contractor may not financially be in a position to carry out the repairs, resulting in delays which cause problems for both the Contractor and the Employer.

A Contractor will obtain cover for loss of or damage to the Works within a CAR policy, the nature of which was outlined in Chapter 1and which is dealt with in more detail in Chapter 10.For works which involve the erection of plant with construction of buildings, the equivalent is an EAR (Erection All Risks) insurance policy. It will be recognised that some contracts (such as power stations or petrochemical installations) may therefore be insured by either CAR or EAR policies when they have significant plant or civil engineering elements respectively. In this text, EAR policies are not mentioned further.

Cover under a CAR Policy is not provided for uninsurable risks, or matter which could be the foreseeable consequence of the way a Contractor proceeds with undertaking Works (i.e. an insurer will not underwrite purely commercial risks taken by a Contractor), or for Employer's risks (whether defined formally as ‘Excepted’ risks or otherwise). The policy will normally continue in force until the Works or completed portions are handed over to an Employer, and for damage caused to Works by the presence of a Contractor during a maintenance/defects correction period.

The insurance requirements of the various ICE Conditions of Contract are described below.

ICE 6, Clause 21

Wording of Clause 21(1) — Insurance of Works, etc.

‘The Contractor shall without limiting his or the Employer’s obligation and responsibilities under Clause 20 insure in the joint names of the Contractor and the Employer the Works together with materials plant and equipment for incorporation therein to the full replacement cost plus an additional 10% to cover any additional costs that may arise incidental to the rectification of any loss or damage including professional fees cost of demolition and removal of debris.’

Commentary on Clause 21(1)

The fact that a Contractor is obliged to arrange insurance does not detract from ‘his obligations and responsibilities for the care of the Works under Clause 20’.

As already noted, insurance in ‘joint names’ is a co-insurance of the Employer and the Contractor.

‘The Works together with materials plant equipment for incorporation therein’ must be insured ‘for the full replacement cost plus an additional 10% to cover any additional costs that may arise incidental to the rectification of any loss or damage including professional fees cost of demolition and removal of debris’. In calculating the full replacement cost, it would be incorrect simply to insure for the contract value. Suppose the Contractor is carrying out a contract worth £10 million, that the contract is expected to last for three years, but that the whole Works are completely destroyed by fire shortly before handover. Allowing for inevitable delays, such as for removing debris, it may be some time before the Contractor can start reconstruction. Certainly it is unlikely that the three-year contract could be completed at the original figure of £10 million. What the insured parties require is a sum of money to enable the Contractor to rebuild the damaged works, bearing in mind the increased cost. Thus, in calculating the sum insured, a Contractor has to estimate the future increased cost of rebuilding. Although some Contract Works insurance policies have a small inflation factor built into them, a prudent Contractor should always make sure that the sum insured represents the true cost of rebuilding (plus the 10% required by the Clause).

In cases where a total loss is an unreasonable expectation, then it should be made permissible under a contract for a Contractor to insure not on a ‘full value’ basis but on what is known as a ‘first loss’ basis (subject to the wording of Clause 21 being altered to permit this). In other words, the Contractor calculates what the maximum loss is likely to be and insures for that figure (plus 10%). This situation may occur when the Works are to be built in sections over a wide area and it is not possible to visualise total loss of all the sections at the same time from the same cause.

Insurance is not contractually required under Clause 21 for Contractor’s Equipment (including temporary buildings) which will not be part of the Permanent Works. A Contractor may insure such items as part of the CAR policy but separate policies for Equipment are also available.

Wording of Clause 21(2) — Extent of cover

  1. ‘(a) The insurance required under sub-clause (1) of this Clause shall cover the Employer and the Contractor against all loss or damage from whatsoever cause arising other than the Excepted Risks defined in Clause 20(2) from the Works Commencement Date until the date of issue of the relevant Certificate of Substantial Completion.
  2. (b) The insurance shall extend to cover any loss or damage arising during the Defects Correction Period from a cause occurring prior to the issue of any Certificate of Substantial Completion and any loss or damage occasioned by the Contractor in the course of any operation carried out by him for the purpose of complying with his obligations under Clauses 49 and 50.
  3. (c) Nothing in this Clause shall render the Contractor liable to insure against the necessity for the repair or reconstruction of any work constructed with materials or workmanship not in accordance with the requirements of the Contract unless the Bill of Quantities shall provide a special item for this insurance.
  4. (d) Any amounts not insured or not recovered from insurers whether as excesses carried under the policy or otherwise shall be borne by the Contractor or the Employer in accordance with their respective responsibilities under Clause 20.’

Commentary on Clause 21(2)

The cover must be ‘against all loss or damage from whatever cause arising other than the Excepted Risks’.

The insurance must be in force ‘from the Works Commencement Date until the date of issue of the relevant Certificate of Substantial Completion’.

Insurance must also cover ‘loss or damage ⋯ during the Defects Correction Period ⋯ occasioned by the Contractor ⋯ for the purpose of complying with his obligations under Clauses 49 and 50’.

Under Clause 21(2)(c), a Contractor is not obliged ‘to insure ⋯ against ⋯ repair or reconstruction of any work constructed with materials or workmanship not in accordance with the requirements of the Contract unless the Bill of Quantities shall provide a special item for this insurance’. This means that insurance is not required against the cost of making good faulty or defective workmanship or materials. Insurers take this to be part of a Contractor’s trade risks and not, generally speaking, insurable as part of a normal Contractor’s Works policy. Uninsured losses for which a Contractor is responsible under Clause 20, including policy deductibles and excess over limits of indemnity, must still be borne by the Contractor.

ICE Design and Construct, Clause 21

The only notable difference in wording between Clause 21 of ICE Design and Construct and Clause 21 of ICE 6 occurs in sub-clause 21(2)(c). The final phrase of the latter states that insurance is not required for defective workmanship or materials ‘unless the Bill of Quantities shall provide a special item for this ‘insurance’, whereas ICE Design and Construct uses the words ‘unless the Contract otherwise requires’. ICE Design and Construct is thus apparently less limited than ICE 6 in that it recognises that Employer requirements for such insurance can be stated in sections of contract documents other than Bills of Quantities, e.g. in the Conditions of Contract. An insurance requirement for defective work cover, if it exists, is therefore no different between the two forms, but a Contractor may need to look more widely afield in the contract documents in order to determine whether it exists. However, although there is little difference in the wording of Clause 21, the amendment to Clause 20(2) means that the insurance must include damage caused by the Contractor’s design.

ICE Minor Works,Clause 10.1

Wording of Clause 10.1 — Insurance of the Works

  1. ‘(1) If so stated in the Appendix the Contractor shall maintain insurance in the joint names of the Employer and the Contractor in respect of the Works (including for the purpose of Clause 10 any unfixed materials or other things delivered to the Site for incorporation therein) to their full value against all loss or damage from whatever cause arising (other than the Excepted Risks) for which he is responsible under the terms of the Contract.
  2. (2) Such insurance shall be effected in such a manner that the Employer and the Contractor are covered for the period stipulated in Clause 3.2 and are also covered for loss or damage arising during the Defects Correction Period from such cause occurring prior to the commencement of the Defects Correction Period and for any loss or damage occasioned by the Contractor in the course of any operation carried out by him for the purpose of complying with his obligations under Clauses 4.7 and 5.2.
  3. (3) The Contractor shall not be liable to insure against the necessity for the repair or reconstruction of any work constructed with materials or workmanship not in accordance with the requirements of the Contract.
  4. (4) Any amounts not insured or not recovered from insurers whether as excesses carried under the policy or otherwise shall be borne by the Contractor or the Employer in accordance with their respective responsibilities under Clauses 3.2 and 3.3.’

Commentary on Clause 10.1

Insurance to cover damage to the Works is not compulsory. This reflects the fact that minor works contractors will usually rely solely upon the cover provided by their annual all-risks policies. Nevertheless, an Employer has the option of requiring a Contractor to take out works insurance, by making an appropriate amendment to the Appendix to the Conditions of Contract. However, it might prove administratively easier to handle, cheaper overall, more likely to provide comprehensive cover and less likely to result in disputes as to who is liable, if an Employer takes out specific project insurance in the Employer’s name covering the activities of the Contractor. An Employer might also exercise the latter option to avoid asking several minor works contractors on the same project to take out separate insurances.

NEC,Clause 84 — Insurance cover

Clause 84 in part relates to insurance of the Works by a Contractor.

Wording of Clause 84.1

‘The Contractor provides the insurances stated in the Insurance Table except any insurance which the Employer is to provide as stated in the Contract Data. The Contractor provides additional insurances as stated in the Contract Data. ’

Insurance Table

Insurance against Minimum amount of cover or minimum limit of indemnity
Loss of or damage to the works, Plant and Materials. The replacement cost, including the amount stated in the Contract Data for the replacement of any Plant and Materials provided by the Employer.
Loss of or damage to Equipment. The replacement cost.

Wording of Clause 84.2

‘The insurances are in the joint names of the Parties and provide cover for events which are at the Contractor's risk from the starting date until the Defects Certificate has been issued.’

Contract Data, Part one — Data provided by the Employer

One of the ‘Optional Statements’ is entitled ‘If the Employer is to provide Plant and Materials’.

The wording of the Optional Statement is as follows.

‘The insurance against loss of or damage to the works, Plant and Materials is to include for Plant and Materials provided by the Employer for an amount of ’

Another of the ‘Optional Statements’ enables the Employer to provide insurance of the Works, Plant, Materials or Equipment instead of the Contractor. It requires the Employer to fill in details of the indemnity to be provided and the applicable deductibles.

Commentary on Clauses 84.1 and 84.2

The insurances under these Clauses are to cover Contractor’s risks.

A Contractor must insure against loss of or damage to the Works, Plant and Materials (i.e. those items included in the Works) at replacement cost: this includes any replacement cost stated in the Contract Data by the Employer for Plant and Materials supplied by the Employer.

A Contractor must also insure for a replacement cost to cover loss of or damage to Equipment (i.e. Contractor's equipment used but not incorporated in the Works).

A Contractor must supply any additional insurance stated by the Employer in the Contract Data.

Alternatively the Employer must insure the Works, Plant and Materials, and/or Equipment if he so states in the Contract data.

The insurances must be in the joint names of the Employer and Contractor (i.e. as co-insureds) and extend from the starting date until the date of issue of the Defects Certificate.
