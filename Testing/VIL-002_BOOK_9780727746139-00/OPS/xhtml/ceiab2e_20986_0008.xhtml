8 Care of the Works within ICE Conditions of Contract

Under ICE conditions of contract a Contractor has an obligation to complete and to hand over to an Employer an undamaged project.

ICE 6, Clause 20

The three paragraphs of this Clause apportion responsibility for loss or damage to the Works between the Contractor and the Employer. The following contractual definitions are relevant.

  • • ‘Permanent Works’ means the permanent works to be constructed and completed in accordance with the contract.
  • • ‘Temporary Works’ means all temporary works of every kind required in or about the construction and completion of the Works.
  • • ‘Works’ means the Permanent Works together with the Temporary Works.

Wording of Clause 20(1) — Care of the Works

  1. ‘(a) The Contractor shall save as in paragraph (b) hereof and subject to sub-clause (2) of this Clause take full responsibility for the care of the Works and materials plant and equipment for incorporation therein from the Works Commencement Date until the date of issue of a Certificate of Substantial Completion for the whole of the Works when the responsibility for the said care shall pass to the Employer.
  2. (b) If the Engineer issues a Certificate of Substantial Completion for any Section or part of the Permanent Works the Contractor shall cease to be responsible for the care of that Section or part from the date of issue of such Certificate of Substantial Completion when the responsibility for the care of that Section or part shall pass to the Employer.
  3. (c) The Contractor shall take full responsibility for the care of any outstanding work and materials plant and equipment for incorporation therein which he undertakes to finish during the Defects Correction Period until such outstanding work has been completed.’

Commentary on Clause 20(1)

The Contractor is fully responsible for the care of the Works from the Works Commencement Date until the date of issue of a Certificate of Substantial Completion. (This obligation does not prevent a Contractor from seeking contribution from any negligent third party who might be considered actually responsible, for the costs of any loss or damage that the Contractor has had to make good. For example, Clause 4(4) provides that a Contractor remains liable for all sub-contractor’s work.)

The Certificate of Substantial Completion can apply to any Section or part of the Permanent Works. After issue, the care of that Section passes to the Employer. The employer must then make independent arrangements, which might include insurance, to cover those new areas of risk.

However, the Contractor remains responsible for the care of any outstanding work agreed to be undertaken during the Defects Correction Period.

Wording of Clause 20(2) — Excepted Risks

‘The Excepted Risks for which the Contractor is not liable are loss or damage to the extent that it is due to

  1. (a) the use of occupation by the Employer his agents servants or other contractors (not being employed by the Contractor) of any part of the Permanent Works
  2. (b) any fault defect error or omission in the design of the Works (other than a design provided by the Contractor pursuant to his obligations under the Contract)
  3. (c) riot war invasion act of foreign enemies or hostilities (whether war be declared or not)
  4. (d) civil war rebellion revolution insurrection or military or usurped power
  5. (e) ionizing radiations or contamination by radioactivity from any nuclear fuel radioactive toxic explosive or other hazardous properties of any explosive nuclear assembly or nuclear component thereof and
  6. (f) pressure waves caused by aircraft or other aerial devices travelling at sonic or supersonic speeds.’

Commentary on Clause 20(2)

Excepted Risks are common exceptions in insurance policies (except that riot is normally insured under policies issued in England, Scotland and Wales, the Isle of Man and the Channel Isles but not Northern Ireland). The onus of proving that an Excepted Risk operates is upon a Contractor.

The use or occupation of Permanent Works by the Employer or his agents, servants or other contractors, resulting in injury, loss or damage to the Works, is an Excepted Risk.

‘A fault, defect, error or omission in the design of the Works (other than a Contractor’s design)’ is also an Excepted Risk retained by an Employer, who can sue whoever is considered to be responsible. The words ‘fault defect error or omission’ have a wider interpretation than ‘negligent’ design. Thus the exception will apply to a faulty design by anyone (other than a design for which the Contractor is responsible) even if it was not a negligent one.

Wording of Clause 20(3) — Rectification of loss or damage

  1. ‘(a) In the event of any loss or damage to
    1. (i) the Works or any Section or part damage thereof or
    2. (ii) materials plant or equipment for incorporation therein

    2. while the Contractor is responsible for the care thereof (except as provided in sub-clause (2) of this Clause) the Contractor shall at his own cost rectify such loss or damage so that the Permanent Works conform in every respect with the provisions of the Contract and the Engineer’s instructions. The contractor shall also be liable for any loss or damage to the Works occasioned by him in the course of any operations carried out by him for the purpose of complying with his obligations under Clauses 49 and 50.

  2. (b) Should any such loss or damage arise from any of the Excepted Risks defined in sub-clause (2) of this Clause the Contractor shall if and to the extent required by the Engineer rectify the loss or damage at the expense of the Employer.
  3. (c) In the event of loss or damage arising from an Excepted Risk and a risk for which the Contractor is responsible under sub-clause (l)(a) of this Clause then the Engineer shall when determining the expense to be borne by the Employer under the Contract apportion the cost of rectification into that part caused by the Excepted Risk and that part which is the responsibility of the Contractor.’

Commentary on Clause 20(3)

Other than damage caused by Excepted Risks, any damage, loss or injury from any cause whatsoever to the Works, while the Contractor is responsible for them, must be put right at the Contractor’s own expense. The intention of the drafters seems to be that ‘any cause whatsoever’ includes any non-excepted negligence on an Employer’s part, and that the Contractor will not be able to sue the Employer where the Employer was at fault. (ICE 6 Insurance Clauses (see Chapter 9) require insurance for the joint benefit of Contractor and Employer so that whoever is responsible will be protected by insurance.)

Where loss or damage arises from any of the Excepted Risks, the Contractor must rectify the loss or damage if required to do so by the Engineer, at the expense of the Employer.

If a Contractor causes damage to the Works during or after the Defects Correction Period (the reference in Clause 20(3) to obligations under Clauses 49 and 50), the Contractor is liable also for that damage.

ICE Design and Construct, Clause 20

The wording is the same as ICE 6 with the following exceptions.

Clause 20(1) — Care of the Works

  1. (a) In sub-clause (a) the superfluous word ‘Works’ is deleted from before the phrase ‘…from the Works Commencement Date …’
  2. (b) In sub-clause (b) the word ‘Engineer’ is replaced by the words ‘Employer’s Representative’. This has no particular insurance implications. Following the final word ‘Employer’ are the additional words ‘Provided also that the Contractor shall remain responsible for any damage to such completed work caused by or as a result of his other activities on site’.

This is a common-sense clarification to recognise the fact that the Contractor would be responsible for damage the Contractor incurs to works that have been handed over to an Employer.

Clause 20(2) — Excepted Risks

  1. (a) In sub-clause (b) the words ‘for which the Contractor is not responsible under the Contract’ replace the parenthetic words ‘(other than a design provided by the Contractor pursuant to his obligations under the Contract)’. In effect these words mean the same, the Design and Construct version being slightly simpler. However, when Clause 20(2)(b) is rewritten without the double negative at present within it, it states that ‘… the Contractor is liable [for] loss and damage to the extent that they are due to any fault defect error or omission in the design of the Works for which the Contractor is responsible under the Contract’.

Clause 20(3) — Rectification of loss or damage

  1. (a) In sub-clause (a) the words ‘and the Engineer’s instructions’ have been deleted after the words ‘… provisions of the Contract’. As a requirement of the Contract is to comply with the Engineer’s instructions, this is a deletion of superfluous words.
  2. (b) In sub-clause (c), the words ‘then the cost of rectification shall be apportioned accordingly’ replace the previous words ‘then the Engineer shall, when determining the expense to be borne by the Employer under the Contract, apportion the cost of rectification into that part caused by the Excepted Risk and that part which is the Responsibility of the Contractor’. Again the substitution is a simplification of the existing words with no particular insurance implications.

ICE Minor Works, Clause 3.2

Wording of Clause 3.2 — Care of the Works

  1. ‘(1) The Contractor shall take full responsibility for the care of the Works from the starting date until 14 days after the Engineer issues a Certificate of Practical Completion for the whole of the Works pursuant to Clause 4.5.
  2. (2) If the Engineer issues a Certificate of Practical Completion in respect of any part of the Works before completion of the whole of the Works the Contractor shall cease to be responsible for care of that part of the Works 14 days thereafter and the responsibility for its care shall then pass to the Employer.
  3. (3) The Contractor shall take full responsibility for the care of any outstanding work which he has undertaken to finish during the Defects Correction Period until such outstanding work is complete.’

Commentary on Clause 3.2

It will be seen that the main provisions are similar to ICE 6 except that ICE Minor Works requires a Contractor to care for the works for 14 days longer. This means that any Contractor’s works insurance will also be required for 14 days longer, with the Employer having a correspondingly increased time within which to arrange his own cover.

NEC, Clause 81 — The Contractor’s risks

Wording of Clause 81.1

‘From thestarting date until the Defects Certificate has been issued the risks which are not carried by theEmployer are carried by the Contractor. ’

NEC, Clause 82 — Repairs

Wording of Clause 82.1

‘Until the Defects Certificate has been issued and unless otherwise instructed by the Project Manager the Contractor promptly replaces loss of and repairs damage to the works, Plant and Materials.’

NEC, Clause 80 — Employer’s risks

Part-wording of Clause 80.1

‘The Employer’s risk are

  • — Loss of or damage to Plant and Materials supplied to theContractor by the Employer, or by Others on the Employer’s behalf, until theContractor has received and accepted them.
  • — Loss of or damage to the works, Plant and Materials due to
    • — war, civil war, rebellion, revolution, insurrection, military or usurped power,
    • — strikes, riots and civil commotion not confined to the Contractor’s employees,
    • — radioactive contamination.
  • — Loss of or damage to the parts of the works taken over by the Employer except loss or damage occurring before the issue of the Defects Certificate which is due to
    • — a Defect which existed at take over,
    • — an event occurring before take over which was not itself an Employer’s risk or
    • — the activities of the Contractor on the Site after take over
  • — Loss of or damage to theworks and any Equipment, Plant and Materials retained on Site by the Employer after a termination except loss and damage due to the activities of the Contractor on the Site after termination.
  • — Additional Employer’s risks stated in the Contract Data’.

Commentary on Clauses 80.1, 81.1 and 82.1

In the context of these Clauses, ‘Plant and Materials’ are those items incorporated into the Works, whereas ‘Equipment’ is equipment used by the Contractor and not incorporated into the Works.

There are several critical dates relating to the care of the Works, Plant and Materials, and Equipment. These are the ‘starting date’, the date(s) when plant and materials supplied by the Employer (or on the Employer’s behalf) are received and accepted by the Contractor, the taking-over date, and the issue date for the Defects Certificate.

The Employer remains responsible for loss or damage to Plant and Materials supplied by the Employer until the contractor receives and accepts them.

The Employer is always responsible for loss or damage due to war, strikes, radioactive contamination etc., no matter at what time during the contract they occur.

The Employer becomes responsible for loss or damage after taking over the works, except for certain matters which arose before the issue of the Defects Certificate.

The Employer generally becomes responsible for loss or damage to the works and any Employer-retained Equipment, Plant and Materials on site after a contract termination.

The Employer can agree to accept responsibility for additional risks by stating them in the Contract Data.

Risks that are not the Employer’s rest with the Contractor. The Contractor is responsible for promptly replacing loss or repairing damage to the Works, Plant and Materials until the Defects Certificate is issued, unless otherwise advised by the Project Manager. This includes matters which are at the Employer’s risk.
