6 Liability to employees within ICE Conditions of Contract

Previous text has described how ICE Conditions of Contract require a Contractor to indemnify an Employer against claims in respect of death or injury to any person, where ‘any person’ includes third parties and an Employer’s and Contractor’s employees.

However, most ICE Conditions of Contract do not require a Contractor to insure specifically against liability to Contractor’s employees. This is because, in the UK, employee liability insurance is compulsory in law anyway up to a stated minimum amount, by virtue of the Employers’ Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969. Where ICE Conditions of Contract are to be used outside the UK, any statutory requirements for such insurance should be determined and appropriate cover obtained.

The following paragraphs give details of the requirements of the various ICE Conditions of Contract.

ICE 6, Clause 24

Wording of Clause 24 – Accident or injury to work people

‘The Employer shall not be liable for or in respect of any damages or compensation payable at law in respect or in consequence of any accident or injury to any operative or other person in the employment of the Contractor or any of his sub-contractors save and except to the extent that such accident or injury results from or is contributed to by any act or default of the Employer his agents or servants and the Contractor shall indemnify and keep indemnified the Employer against all such damages and compensation (save and except as aforesaid) and against all claims demands proceedings costs charges and expenses whatsoever in respect thereof or in relation thereto’.

Commentary on Clause 24

Accidents to workmen and other people in the employment of a contractor or a contractor’s sub-contractors are included implicitly in Clause 22 of ICE 6 (i.e. the reference to ‘any person’ in Clause 22(l)(a)). Since Clause 24 is the more specific, liability matters would normally be dealt with under Clause 24.

The Contractor has to indemnify the Employer against all damages and compensation paid by him and legal costs and expenses so incurred unless ‘contributed to by any act of default of the Employer his agents or servants’.

The intention of Clause 24 is to deal with the Contractor’s employees, i.e. persons under a contract of service or apprenticeship. As described earlier, those liabilities are the subject of compulsory Employers’ Liability insurance, with the Contractor in this instance being the Employer concerned. However, the wording of Clause 24 also embraces accident or injury to persons in the employment of any Contractor-appointed sub-contractor. The latter matters would be covered by a Contractor’s Public Liability insurance. The result is that a Contractor looking for insurance protection against the indemnity given under Clause 24 will require cover under both Employers’ and Public Liability policies.

There is no contractual requirement under Clause 24 for a Contractor to insure liability for employees. Employees are also excluded from the requirement to obtain third party insurance in Clause 23(1) (see Chapter 4). One advantage of a contractual requirement (as opposed to only a statutory requirement) is that it would enable an Employer to demand that a Contractor should produce evidence and details of employee liability cover and of continuing payment of premiums. A contractual requirement can be introduced by an additional appropriate contract condition. In this way an Employer can then require evidence of insurance and can determine that the law has been complied with and that there are no restrictions or limitations on the cover, e.g. excluding hazardous works such as tunnelling or demolition where that is appropriate to the Works.

What constitutes an ‘employee’ under the Employers’ Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act is discussed further in Chapter 7.

ICE Design and Construct,Clause 24

The wording is identical to ICE 6.

ICE Minor Works

There is no specific requirement expressly stated in ICE Minor Works for insurance to cover accidents or injuries to employees. As already described, this is because such insurance is a statutory requirement of all employers and hence is compulsory anyway. However, Clause 10.3 requires insurance against damage, loss or injury for which the Contractor is liable under Clause 10.2 (i.e. ‘to any person or property whatsoever ⋯’ which would include employees.) This non-specific contract requirement for employee insurance thus enables an Employer to require a Contractor to produce the policy and evidence that premiums are being paid, as discussed previously.

NEC, Clause 84 – Insurance cover

Clause 84 in part relates to insurance to cover a Contractor’s liability to employees.

Wording of Clause 84.1

‘The Contractor provides the insurances stated in the Insurance Table except any insurance which the Employer is to provide as stated in the Contract Data. The Contractor provides the additional insurances as stated in the Contract Data’.

Insurance Table

Insurance against Minimum amount of cover or minimum limit of indemnity
Liability for death of or bodily injury to employees of the Contractor arising out of and in the course of their employment in connection with this contract. The greater of the amount required by the applicable law and the amount stated in the Contract Data for any one event.

Wording of Clause 84.2

‘The insurances are in the joint names of the Parties and provide cover for events which are at the Contractor’s risk from the starting date until the Defects Certificate has been issued.

Contract Data, Part one – Data provided by the Employer

Paragraph 8 in part relates to Employer requirements for the Contractor’s insurance cover of liability to employees. The relevant part-wording is:

‘The amount of the minimum limit of indemnity for insurance in respect of death of or bodily injury to employees of the Contractor arising out of and in the course of their employment in connection with this contract is ⋯’

Another of the ‘Optional Statements’ enables the Employer to provide insurance against liability to Contractor’s employees instead of the Contractor. It requires the Employer to fill in details of the indemnity to be provided and any applicable deductibles.

Commentary on Clauses 84.1, 84.2 and Paragraph 8 of the Contract Data

The Contractor must provide insurance against liability for injury to, or death of, the Contractor’s employees.

The Employer states the minimum amount of indemnity required per event, but the Table acknowledges that there may also be a statutory limit placed by legislation where the Contractor acts as an employer, e.g. the UK Employers’ Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969.

There remains an option for an Employer to provide a Contractor’s insurance cover of employees. This could be relevant in territories where there was no statutory obligation for compulsory insurance cover to be obtained by employers.

The joint names policy permits an Employer to rely on the insurance cover where a Contractor’s employee claims against the Employer or both Employer and Contractor, instead of solely the Contractor.
