12. Sub-Contractors and Sub-Contract agreements

As discussed previously, in claims for personal injury or damage to property the Contractor gives an indemnity to the Employer under the Construction Contract conditions. The Contractor is obliged also to indemnify the Employer in respect of injury or damage which arises from the Sub-Contractor's activities. The FCEC Sub-Contract is intended for use in conjunction with ICE 6, the FCEC D&C Sub-Contract is for use in conjunction with ICE Design and Contruct, and the NEC Subcontract is appropriate for the New Engineering Contract system. The subcontract forms provide that the Sub-Contractor shall indemnify the Contractor against certain kinds of injury, loss or damage and require insurance by the Sub-Contractor.

It will be noted that the term ‘Sub-Contractor’ is used in FCEC Sub-Contract and FCEC D&C Sub-Contract and the term ‘Subcontractor’ in NEC Subcontract. There are other similar differences. Commentary has attempted to follow the appropriate terminology in each case.

FCEC Sub-Contract Clauses 12 and 14

Wording of Clause 12 — Indemnities

  1. ‘(1) The Sub-Contractor shall at all times indemnify the Contractor against all liabilities to other persons (including the servants and agents of the Contractor or Sub-Contractor) for bodily injury, damage to property or other loss which may arise out of or in consequence of the execution, completion or maintenance of the Sub-Contract Works and against all costs, charges and expensesthat may beoccasioned to the Contractor by the claims of such persons.

    2. Provided always that the Contractor shall not be entitled to the benefit of this indemnity in respect of any liability or claim if he is entitled by the terms of the Main Contract to be indemnified in respect thereof by the Employer.

    Provided further that the Sub-Contractor shall not be bound to indemnify the Contractor against any such liability or claim ifthe injury, damage or loss in question was caused solely by the wrongful acts or omissions of the Contractor, his servants or agents.

  2. (2) The Contractor shall indemnify the Sub-Contractor against all liability and claims against which the Employer by the terms of the Main Contract undertakes to indemnify the Contractor and to the like extent, but no further.’

Commentary on Clause 12

The indemnity applies in respect of injury, damage or loss which arises ‘out of or in consequence of the execution, completion or maintenance of the Sub-Contract Works … ’. In the practical sense that will include all incidents on or about the site of the Sub-Contract Works and where Sub-Contract Work is undertaken off-site. It is no answer to a claim for indemnity by the Contractor to say thatthe injury arises out of employee negligence and not out of or in consequence of the execution of the Sub-Contract Works.

The Contractor is not entitled to the Sub-Contractor’s indemnity if the Contractor is already entitled to a similar indemnity from the Employer under the Main Contract and, under Clause 12(2), passes on to the Sub-Contractor the benefit of that indemnity.

The Sub-Contractor is not bound to indemnify the Contractor where injury or damage is caused ‘solely’ by the wrongful act or omission of the Contractor, his servants or agents. The use of the word solely means that the Contractor must be 100% at fault. Where there is 90% of fault on theContractor and 10% on the Sub-Contractor, the Sub-Contractor still has to indemnify the Contractor for the full amount.

The Sub-Contractor should arrange Employers’ Liability and Public Liability insurances which will also provide indemnity against the liability assumed under Clause 12.

Wording of Clause 14(1) — Liability insurance

‘The Sub-Contractor shall effect insurance against such risks as are specified in Part I of the Fifth Schedule hereto and in such sums and for the benefit of such persons as are specified therein and unless the said Fifth Schedule otherwise provides, shall maintain such insurance from the time that the Sub-Contractor first enters upon the Site for the purpose of executing the Sub-Contract Works until he has finally performed his obligations under Clause 13 (Outstanding Work and Defects).’

Commentary on Clause 14(1)

The ‘Notes for the guidance of Contractors on the completion of the schedules’ at the back of FCEC Sub-Contract say no more than that Part I of the fifth schedule ‘should specify insurances to be effected by the Sub-Contractor’. Having regard to the provisions of Clause 14(2), however, it is suggested that Part 1 of the fifth schedule should refer to Employers’ Liability, Public Liability and Motor insurance for the Sub-Contractor’s own benefit and, if the Sub-Contractor is not to have the benefit of the Contractor’s policy, insurance of the Sub-Contract Works, constructional plant, temporary works, materials and other objects belonging to the Sub-Contractor.

Wording of Clause 14(2) — Insurance of the Sub-Contract Works

‘The Contractor shall maintain in force until such time as the Main Works have been substantially completed or ceased to be at his risk under the Main Contract, the policy of insurance specified in Part II of the Fifth Schedule hereto. In the event of the Sub-Contract Works, or any Sub-Contractor’s Equipment, Temporary Works, materials or other things belonging to the Sub-Contractor being destroyed or damaged during such period in such circumstances that a claim is established in respect thereof under the said policy, then the Sub-Contractor shall be paid the amount of such claim, or the amount of his loss, whichever is the less, and shall apply such sum in replacing or repairing that which was destroyed or damaged. Save as aforesaid the Sub-Contract Works shall be at the risk of the SubContractor until the Main Works have been substantially completed under the Main Contract, or if the Main Works are to be completed by sections, until the last of the sections in which the Sub-Contract Works are comprised has been substantially completed, and the SubContractor shall make good all loss of or damage occurring to the Sub-Contract Works prior thereto at his own expense.’

Commentary on Clause 14(2)

The Guidance Notes published as part of FCEC Sub-Contract say of Part II of the fifth schedule:

‘Part 2 should specify the policy of insurance which the Contractor is effecting in pursuance of Clause 21 of the Main Contract Conditions, if it is intended that the Sub-Contractor shall have the benefit thereof. In such cases his interest should be noted either generally or specifically on the policy and this Part of the schedule should so state. If the Sub-Contractor is not to have any benefit under the policy of the Contractor, then that Part should be marked ‘not applicable’.

With respect to the author of the Guidance Note, where Clause 14(2) applies and the Copntractor insures the Works, the better course would be for the Sub-Contractor to be named in the policy as an insured. The noting of an interest on a policy is no more than an acknowledgement by the Insurer of notice of an interest adverse to that of the named Insured. If the Sub-Contractor is not a named Insured he cannot claim on the policy other than through the Contractor, has no rights against the Insurers and may have no right to receive payment of any policy monies other than through the Contractor.

If the Contractor does insure, the Sub-Contractor should ensure that the Contractor’s policy provides adequate cover in respect of the Sub-Contract Works. The Sub-Contract Works are at the risk of the Sub-Contractor, save to the extent that they are insured by the Contractor’s policy, until substantial completion of the Main Works — which may be some time after the completion of the Sub-Contract Works and after the SubContractor has left the site. Any shortfall in the Contractor’s insurance cover will, therefore, be to the disadvantage of the Sub-Contractor.

FCEC D&C Sub-Contract

The provisions of Clauses 12 and 14 are identical to those in FCEC Sub-Contract. The ‘Notes for the guidance of Contractors on the completion of the schedules’ at the back of FCEC D&C Sub-Contract say of the fifth schedule:

‘Reference should be made to Clause 14 (Insurances) of the SubContract. In completing the two parts of this Schedule the parties shouldtake care to ensure that all insurances required by the Main Contract are effected by one or other of them and that there is no unnecessary duplication of insurance.

Part I should specify insurances to be effected by the SubContractor. Any Professional Indemnity insurance requirements should be stated.’

The mention of Professional Indemnity insurance arises because the Main Contract and the Sub-Contract will have a design element in respect of the permanent works whereas ICE 6 and FCEC Sub-Contract will not. The terms of this guidance note are thought preferable to that which appearsin FCEC Sub-Contract.

NEC Subcontract, Clause 80 — The Employer’s and Contractor’s risks

Wording of Clause 80.1

‘80.1 The Employer’s and Contractor’s risk are

  1. ⋆Claims, proceedings, compensation and costs payable which are due to
    1. ⋆use or occupation of the Site by the works or for the purpose of the works which is the unavoidable result of the works,
    2. ⋆negligence, breach of statutory duty or interference with any legal right by the Employer or the Contractor or by any person employed by or contracted to them except the Subcontractor or
    3. ⋆a fault of the Employer or Contractor or a fault in their designs.
  2. ⋆Loss of or damage to Plant and Materials supplied to the Subcontractor by the Employer or Contractor or by Others on the Employer’s or Contractor’s behalf, until the Subcontractor has received and accepted them.
  3. ⋆Loss of or damage to the works, Plant and Materials due to
    1. ⋆war, civil war, rebellion, revolution, insurrection, military or usurped power,
    2. ⋆strikes, riots and civil commotion not confined to the Subcontractor’s employees,
    3. ⋆radioactive contamination.
  4. ⋆Loss of or damage to parts of the subcontract works taken over by the Employer or Contractor, except loss or damage occurring before the issue of the Defects Certificate which is due to
    1. ⋆Defect which existed at take over,
    2. ⋆an event occurring before take over which was not itself an Employer’s or Contractor’s risk or
    3. ⋆the activities of the Subcontractor on the Site after take over.
  5. ⋆Loss of or damage to the subcontract works and any Equipment, Plant and Materials retained on Site by the Employer or Contractor after a termination, except loss or damage due to the activities of the Subcontractor on the Site after the termination.
  6. ⋆Additional Employer’s or Contractor’s risks stated in the Subcontract Data’.

NEC Subcontract, Clause 81 — The Subcontractor’s risks

Wording of Clause 81.1

‘81.1 From the subcontract starting date until the Defects Certificate has been issued the risks which are not carried by the Employer or the Contractor are carried by the Subcontractor. ’

NEC Subcontract,Clause 82 — Repairs

Wording of Clause 82.1

‘82.1 Unless the Defects Certificate has been issued and unless otherwise instructed by the Contractor the Subcontractor promptly replaces loss of and repairs damage to the subcontract works, Plant and Materials.’

NEC Subcontract, Clause 83 — Indemnity

Wording of Clauses 83.1 to 83.3

‘83.1 Each Party indemnifies the other against claims, proceedings, compensation and costs due to an event which is at his risk. The Contractor indemnifies the Subcontractor against all claims and liabilities against which the Employer indemnifies the Contractor under the main contract.

83.2 The liability of the Subcontractor to indemnify the Contractor is reduced if events at the Employer’s or Contractor’s risk contributed to the claims, proceedings, compensation and costs. The reduction is in proportion to the extent that events which were at the Employer’s or Contractor’s risk contributed, taking into account the responsibilities of each Party under this subcontract.

83.3 The liability of the Contractor to indemnify the Subcontractor is reduced if events at the Subcontractor’s risk contributed to the claims, proceedings, compensation and costs. The reduction is in proportion to the extent that events which were at the Subcontractor’s risk contributed, taking into account the responsibilities of each Party under this subcontract.’

NEC Subcontract,Clause 84 — Insurance cover

Wording of Clauses 84.1 and 84.2

‘84.1 The Subcontractor provides the insurances stated in the Insurance Table except any insurance which the Employer or the Contractor is to provide as stated in the Subcontract Data. The Contractor provides additional insurances as stated in the Subcontract Data.

Insurance Table

Insurance against Minimum amount of cover or minimum limit of indemnity
Loss of or damage to the subcontract works, Plant and Materials. The replacement cost, including the amount stated in the Subcontract Data for the replacement of any Plant and Materials provided by the Employer or Contractor.
Loss of or damage to Equipment. The replacement cost.
Liability for loss of or damage to property (except the subcontract works, Plant and Materials and Equipment and liability for bodily injury to or death of a person (not an employee of the Subcontractor) caused by activity in connection with this subcontract. The amount stated in the Subcontract Data for any one event with cross liability so that the insurance applies to the parties separately.
Liability for death of or bodily injury to employees of the Subcontractor arising out of and in the course of their employment in connection with this subcontract. The greater of the amount required by the applicable law and the amount stated in the Subcontract Data for any one event.

84.2 The insurances are in the joint names of the Parties and provide cover for events which are at the Subcontractor’s risk from the subcontract starting date until the Defects Certificate has been issued.’

NEC Subcontract,Clause 85 — Insurance policies

Wording of Clauses 85.1 to 85.4

‘85.1 The Subcontractor submits policies and certificates for the insurance which he is to provide to the Contractor for acceptance before the subcontract starting date and afterwards as the Contractor instructs. A reason for not accepting the policies and certificates is that they do not comply with this subcontract.

84.2 Insurance policies include a waiver by the insurers of their subrogation rights against directors and other employees of every insured except where there is fraud.

84.3 The Parties comply with the terms and conditions of the insurance policies.

84.4 Any amount not recovered from an insurer is borne by the Employer or Contractor for events which are at their risk, and by the Subcontractor for events which are at his risk. ’

NEC Subcontract, Clause 86 — If the Subcontractor does not insure

Wording of Clause 86.1

‘86.1 The Contractor may insure the risk which this subcontract requires the Subcontractor to insure if the Subcontractor does not submit a required policy or certificate. The cost of this insurance to the Contractor is paid by the Subcontractor. ’

NEC Subcontract, Clause 87 — Insurance by the Employer or the Contractor

Wording of Clauses 87.1 to 87.3

‘87.1 The Contractor submits policies and certificates for insurances provided by the Employer or the Contractor to the Subcontractor for acceptance before the subcontract starting date and afterwards as the Subcontractor instructs. The Subcontractor accepts thepolicies and certificates if they comply with this subcontract.

87.2 The Subcontractor’s acceptance of an insurance policy or certificate provided by the Employer or Contractor does not change the responsibility of the Employer or Contractor to provide the insurances stated in the Subcontract Data.

87.3 The Subcontractor may insure a risk which this contract requires the Employer or Contractor to insure if the Contractor does not submit a required policy or certificate. The cost of this insurance to the Subcontractor is paid by the Contractor. ’

Commentary on Clauses 80 to 87

Clauses 80 to 87 are ‘Core Clauses’ and there are other options in the NEC Subcontract which are not considered in this text.

The Core Clauses are straightforward: the risks are apportioned between the parties by Clauses 80 and 81. Repairs to the Works, liability for personal injury and damage to property are then dealt with in Clauses 82 and 83 on the basis of that risk apportionment. Clauses 84 and 85 then set out the required minimum insurance cover.
